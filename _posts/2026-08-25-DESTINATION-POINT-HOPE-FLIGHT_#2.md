Last Tuesday, I completed the second leg of this journey. This one took me to Thunder Bay, on the other side of Lake Superior, following its northern shoreline.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/1.png)

This time, the weather conditions were pretty good — better than on the previous flight, at least. There was a broken cloud layer above FL100 and a 10-knot westerly wind throughout the entire route. A quick check that morning had nevertheless shown a risk of thunderstorms on arrival in Thunder Bay, with some localized showers.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/2.jpg)

The METAR at Sault Ste. Marie reported 5-knot winds from a variable direction, which allowed me to choose Runway 05 and make the departure easier, with a left turn after takeoff toward my first waypoint.

I then quickly climbed to FL045, my planned cruising altitude for this flight. Visibility was excellent at that altitude, allowing me to keep Lake Superior on my left and observe the many protected areas on my right throughout the flight, including Lake Superior Provincial Park, the Northern Claybelt Forest Complex, and Pukaskwa National Park.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/3.jpg)

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/4.jpg)

The forests in this region are beautiful, especially toward the end of August. I could already spot a few trees starting to turn yellow.
As the flight progressed, the environment changed considerably. The towns and signs of civilization gradually disappeared, giving way to untouched wilderness. This is exactly what I was looking for in this long and remote journey.
The conditions on arrival in Thunder Bay were not challenging. The wind was 9 knots from 070°, with Runway 07 in use. There were a few scattered cumulus clouds at 3,200 ft and only a low risk of showers.
Once the airport was in sight, I joined a long final for Runway 07. Perfect glide path, no turbulence.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/5.jpg)

But, just to completely break the immersion, MSFS AI traffic decided to do whatever it wanted and entered the runway just as I was crossing the threshold. That forced me to go around.

The second attempt went even worse: there were two aircraft on Runway 07. One King Air was taxiing, while another turboprop seemed to be doing circuits directly on the runway. I simply made a quick little left-right maneuver and landed a few meters farther down the runway.
