Blue water, blue sky

Tuesday afternoon, I flew the second leg of this journey. This one took me to Thunder Bay, on the other side of Lake Superior following its northern shore.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/1.jpg)

Weather conditions were pretty good this time, with broken clouds above FL100 and a 10-knot wind from the west all along the route. A quick check in the morning showed a chance of thunderstorms near Thunder Bay around my ETA, with towering cumulus and rain showers. Fortunately, nothing developed during the flight, and the weather at arrival was good.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/2.jpg)

The Sault Saint Marie METAR reported a 05-knot wind from a variable direction, which allowed me to choose runway 05 and simplify the departure, with a short left turn after takeoff towards my first checkpoint.
I then climbed quickly to FL045, my planned cruising altitude for this flight. Visibility was excellent at that altitude, allowing me to observe Lake Superior on my left, and the many protected areas on my right all along the route, including Lake Superior Provincial Park, The Nothern Claybelt Forest Complex and the Pukaskwa National Park.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/3.jpg)

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/4.jpg)

Forests in this region are beautiful, especially at the end of August. I could already spot some yellowish trees.
Along the route, the environment changed considerably. Cities and signs of civilization gradually disappeared, giving way to wild nature. This is exactly what I was looking for in this long and isolated journey.

Arrival conditions for Thunder Bay were not challenging. The wind was 09 knots from 070°, with Runway 07 in use. Scattered cumulus at 3,200 ft and only a small chance of rain.
Once the airfield was in sight, I set myself up for a long final for Runway 07. Perfect on the glide path, no turbulences.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_02/CONTENU/5.jpg)

But, just to really brake the immersion, MSFS IA traffic decided to act recklessly and entered the runway while I was crossing the threshold. That forced me to go-around.
Second attempt went even worse, two aircraft were on Runway 07: a King Air was taxiing while another turboprop seemed to be doing donuts right on the runway. I just made a quick left – right maneuver and touched downa few feet further down the runway.
