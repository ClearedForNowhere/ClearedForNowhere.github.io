## THE PREPARATION
September 29, 2026

Back to flying and continuing my journey.
I’ve just finished preparing the ninth leg of my journey, out of the 17 theoretically planned before reaching Point Hope. This one will take me to Smithers. No border to cross, no particular overflight to plan, just a beautiful 169 NM navigation over the landscapes of British Columbia.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_09/CONTENU/1.jpg)

I’m planning to leave Prince George and head west toward my first waypoint, the town of Vanderhoof, a small town in central British Columbia. The Nechako River runs through it, and the airport is located north of the town.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_09/CONTENU/2.jpg)
<span class="small-note"><em>© Google Maps</em></span>

I’ll then continue west for around thirty nautical miles to reach the town of Fraser Lake, located right on the shores of Fraser Lake.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_09/CONTENU/3.jpg)
<span class="small-note"><em>© Google Maps</em></span>

From there, I’ll use the Trans-Canada Highway as a visual reference and follow it toward the town of Burns Lake.
To reach WP3, the town of Houston, I’ll take a heading of 272°, which will take me a few nautical miles south of China Nose Mountain, whose summit reaches 4,682 ft.

![Destination Point Hope]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/DPH_FLIGHT_09/CONTENU/4.jpg)

And to finish this navigation, I’ll once again follow the Trans-Canada Highway toward today’s destination: Smithers.
The route follows the Bulkley River through its valley, which is surrounded by varying terrain, including Grouse Mountain to the east and Mount Forster 10 NM to the west.
This promises yet another flight with some amazing scenery!
Smithers Airport is located north of the town, with Kathlyn Lake to the west and the Bulkley River to the east. I shouldn’t have any trouble spotting it from the air.

It’s not a large airport, but it has everything I need to spend a few days there! I’ll also be able to refuel with 100LL, and I’ve spotted a small municipal campground in town. I think I’ll spend a few days camping there. The area definitely looks worth exploring.

Looking at the weather forecast for this evening (UTC), nothing rules out the flight, but the conditions deserve some particular attention:
The terrain will require me to fly at 6,500 ft (VFR altitude rules + the required safety margin above the terrain). However, temperatures are getting lower and lower, with the estimated freezing level around 7,000 ft.
I’ll therefore be flying very close to potential icing conditions, which is something I’d rather avoid. I’ll need to keep a particularly close eye on the situation throughout the flight.
I shouldn’t encounter any precipitation during the flight, but I’m expecting to fly through a fairly thick cloud layer during my third, fourth, and fifth legs.
Combined with the terrain, I’ll need to consider diverting to Houston if necessary, as it will be my last waypoint before reaching the destination.
As for the wind, it will be from the west at an average of 15 knots at 6,500 ft, increasing with altitude.
